The Jewel of the Philippines

El Nido is a tropical paradise renowned for its towering limestone cliffs, turquoise waters, pristine beaches, and spectacular marine life.

As the gateway to the Bacuit Archipelago, it offers an extraordinary escape for travelers seeking adventure, relaxation, and natural beauty. Kayak through hidden lagoons or snorkel among colorful coral reefs; every day reveals another side of the island.

Food & Culture

Fresh from the sea, rooted in island life.

More Than a Beautiful View

El Nido takes its name from the Spanish word for “nest,” after the edible-nest swiftlets that build in the limestone cliffs. Life here has long been shaped by the sea, and fishing remains a way of life for many families.

Local culture blends Cuyonon heritage with other communities across Palawan. Eating at family-run places, hiring local guides and buying from local makers is the best way to give something back.

What to Eat

🐟

Fresh Seafood

Grilled fish, squid and prawns, often cooked fresh in town in the evenings or on the beach during tours.

🍋

Kinilaw

The Filipino take on ceviche: raw fish cured in vinegar or citrus with onion, ginger and chili.

🥜

Palawan Cashews

Palawan is known for its cashews. Pick up a bag as a snack for the boat or a gift to take home.

🍍

Tropical Fruit

Mango, pineapple and fresh fruit shakes are the perfect way to cool down after a day on the water.

Eating responsibly: ask where the catch comes from and skip protected species such as sea turtles and giant clams.

Essential Guide for Visitors

Everything you need to know before your El Nido adventure.

What to Know Details
Getting There Fly directly to El Nido's Lio Airport, or fly into Puerto Princesa and take a land transfer of several hours. Check current flight and van schedules before you book.
Best Time to Visit December to May is generally the drier season, popular for beach days and island-hopping.
Getting Around Tricycles are convenient for short trips around town. Motorbikes and arranged transfers help you reach mainland attractions.
Connectivity Wi-Fi and mobile signal vary by location. Download offline maps and key travel documents before you go.
ATMs & Cash ATMs can be limited or out of cash, and many small businesses are cash-only. Bring enough Philippine pesos for transport, activities, and remote areas.
What to Bring Reef-safe sunscreen, swimwear, comfortable footwear or water shoes, a waterproof bag, and a reusable water bottle.

Good to Know

🌱 Eco-Tourism Fee

Budget for the environmental fee charged for visiting El Nido's protected areas. Rates and payment procedures can change, so confirm the current details with your tour operator before traveling.

📅 Peak Season Advisory

Demand rises during the dry season and holidays. Book accommodation and preferred island tours early to get the dates you want.

♻️ Keep El Nido Beautiful

Bring a reusable water bottle, avoid disposable plastics, use reef-safe sunscreen, and take your waste with you.

Accommodations

Where you stay shapes your trip. Here is how the main areas compare.

Budget to mid-range

El Nido Town

The liveliest base, close to tour operators, restaurants, bars and shops. Hostels, guesthouses and hotels to suit most budgets.

Best for: first-timers & backpackers
Mid-range

Corong-Corong

A calmer stretch of shoreline just outside town with sunset views and a relaxed beachfront feel.

Best for: sunsets & quiet evenings
Remote

Nacpan & Calitang

Beach huts and small resorts on a wide stretch of sand, a road trip from town. Facilities are simpler.

Best for: slow, beachfront stays
Luxury

Island Resorts

Private-island resorts in Bacuit Bay, reached by boat transfer, with lagoons and reefs right on your doorstep.

Best for: splurges & honeymoons

Booking Tips

  • Reserve early for December–May and for holiday weeks.
  • Check the cancellation policy, since weather can change boat plans.
  • Ask about boat transfers, power and Wi-Fi if you are staying outside town.
  • Many places can arrange island-hopping tours, which saves you a step.

Events & Festivals

Time your visit around local celebrations and seasonal highlights.

Dates vary

El Nido's annual town festival, celebrating local culture and heritage with parades, performances and community events.

March–April
(varies yearly)

Holy Week (Semana Santa)

A major holiday in the Philippines. Expect large crowds and full bookings, and note that some businesses reduce hours around Good Friday.

December–January

Christmas & New Year

One of the busiest times of the year, with festive nights in town. Book rooms and tours well in advance.

Dry season
(December–May)

Live Music & Beach Nights

Beach bars and restaurants in town often host live music and sunset gatherings during the busy months.

Events change from year to year. Ask your accommodation or the local tourism office for the latest schedule.
